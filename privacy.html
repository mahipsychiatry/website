Notice of Privacy Practices

This notice describes how medical information about you may be used and disclosed and how you can get access to this information. Please review it carefully.

Who this notice covers

This notice applies to Mahi Psychiatry & Recovery, PLLC and Mahelet Yalew, DNP, PMHNP-BC. I'm required by law to protect the privacy of your health information, to give you this notice of my legal duties and privacy practices, to follow the notice currently in effect, and to tell you if a breach compromises your unsecured health information.

How I use and share your information

Treatment. I use your information to evaluate and treat you, and I may share it with other professionals involved in your care, such as your pharmacy, primary care provider or therapist.

Payment. I share information with your health plan, and with Headway, which handles scheduling and billing for my practice, so your visits can be billed and paid.

Health care operations. I may use your information to run the practice, improve care and meet professional requirements. Vendors that handle information for me, such as my scheduling, billing, electronic record, telehealth and email services, sign agreements requiring them to protect it.

Appointment reminders. I or my scheduling service may contact you by phone, text or email about appointments.

Other uses and disclosures allowed or required by law

I may share your information without your written permission when the law requires or permits it, including:

  • to prevent or lessen a serious and imminent threat to your health or safety, or someone else's
  • to report suspected abuse or neglect of a child, an elderly person or a vulnerable adult, as Virginia law requires
  • for public health activities, such as reporting adverse reactions to medications
  • to health oversight agencies for audits, investigations and licensing
  • in response to a court order, subpoena or other lawful process
  • to law enforcement, coroners and medical examiners, when the law requires it
  • for workers' compensation claims, as the law allows
  • for specialized government functions, such as national security, as the law allows
  • to the Virginia Prescription Monitoring Program, when I prescribe controlled substances

Substance use disorder records

Records of substance use disorder diagnosis and treatment may have extra protection under federal law (42 CFR Part 2). Where Part 2 applies, I won't share those records without your written consent except as that law allows, such as in a medical emergency, for certain audits and program evaluations, or under a qualifying court order. Records protected by Part 2 can't be used or shared in a civil, criminal, administrative or legislative proceeding against you unless you give specific written consent or a court orders it after giving you notice and a chance to be heard. You may give one written consent that covers future uses for treatment, payment and health care operations, and you can revoke it in writing at any time.

Uses that need your written permission

I'll ask for your written authorization before using or sharing psychotherapy notes (if any are kept), using your information for marketing, or selling your information, which I never do. Any other use not described here also requires your written permission. You may revoke an authorization in writing at any time, except where I've already acted on it.

Your rights

  • Get a copy of your records. You can ask for an electronic or paper copy. I'll respond within 30 days and may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee.
  • Ask me to correct your records. If you think something is wrong or incomplete, you can ask in writing. If I say no, I'll tell you why in writing within 60 days.
  • Ask for confidential communications. You can ask me to contact you in a certain way, such as a different phone number or address. I'll agree to reasonable requests.
  • Ask me to limit what I share. I'm not required to agree, except that if you pay for a service in full yourself, you can ask me not to share it with your health plan, and I'll say yes unless the law requires otherwise.
  • Get a list of disclosures. You can ask for a list of times I've shared your information in the past six years, other than for treatment, payment, operations and certain other purposes.
  • Get a paper copy of this notice at any time, even if you agreed to get it electronically.
  • Choose someone to act for you. A legal guardian or someone with your medical power of attorney can exercise your rights.

Complaints

If you think your privacy rights have been violated, you can contact me using the information below. You can also file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Office for Civil Rights, at hhs.gov/ocr/complaints or 1-877-696-6775. I will never retaliate against you for filing a complaint.

Changes to this notice

I can change this notice, and the changes will apply to all information I have about you. The current notice will always be posted on this website and available on request.
